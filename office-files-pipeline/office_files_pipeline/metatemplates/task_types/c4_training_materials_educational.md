# Training Materials & Educational Presentations

**Macro Category:** C — Policy, Procedure & Standards Authoring
**Pattern ID:** C4

## 1. Pattern Description

The worker creates a structured instructional package — slide deck, training guide, coaching document, case study set, or educational workshop materials — designed to teach a specific audience a skill, process, knowledge domain, or behavioral practice. Unlike a reference document or clinical guideline (which records authoritative information), training materials are pedagogically structured: they sequence content to build understanding progressively, include exercises or application activities, calibrate language to the target audience's literacy and role, and embed mechanisms for knowledge assessment (quizzes, discussion prompts, homework assignments). The cognitive core is instructional design: the worker must decompose a topic into learnable chunks, anticipate audience knowledge gaps, select appropriate examples and scenarios, and structure the delivery to produce observable skill or knowledge change. A defining feature is that the training materials can be delivered by someone other than the subject-matter expert who created them.

### Generalizable Work Context
A senior practitioner, manager, or subject-matter expert is tasked with creating reusable training materials for a defined audience facing a knowledge or skill gap. The trigger is usually an organizational performance problem (declining conversion rates, compliance failures, new staff onboarding need) or a regulatory or program change requiring staff education. The training package will be delivered in a session, distributed for self-study, or used as an onboarding resource — and must work without the creator being present to explain it.

## 3. Prompt Construction Template

### Persona Pattern
Assign a role with both subject-matter expertise and a training/coaching responsibility: Senior CSR (training new hires), Regional Director (training team leaders), Financial Advisor (training field advisors), Editor (training editorial staff), Nurse Practitioner (lecturing nursing students), Home Visitor (facilitating parenting program sessions), Retail GM (coaching sales associates). The organizational context should establish the performance gap or knowledge need that motivates the training.

### Scenario Pattern
Name a specific triggering event or gap: declining sales metrics, a new regulatory requirement, a new organizational tool or process being rolled out, an onboarding cohort for new staff, a recurring program curriculum session. Specify the target learner audience precisely (newly onboarded frontline clerks; nursing students in their first clinical year; field advisors managing investment accounts; at-risk mothers in substance abuse recovery programs). Name the training format (live session, self-study reference, weekly session) and approximate time commitment per session.

### Instruction Pattern
Specify the primary deliverable format (PowerPoint, Word document, PDF) and the secondary deliverable if applicable (case studies, exercises, quiz with answer key, homework tracking sheet). List required structural sections by name. Set a slide count or page limit. Specify audience-appropriate language requirements. Indicate whether regulatory sources must be incorporated and, if so, which ones. For dual-deliverable tasks, describe both components with separate content specifications.

### Constraint Injection Points
- **Slide/page count**: Hard limit (e.g., an exact slide count, an upper-bound slide limit, or an approximate page count)
- **Audience calibration**: Literacy level (plain language for clients in recovery; non-technical for editorial staff); role familiarity (new hires vs. experienced practitioners)
- **Required structural elements**: Specific named sections (e.g., an overview establishing why the skill matters, a breakdown of sub-types with examples, a strategies section, a guided-practice section, a conclusion, a homework/follow-up section); structural features (pre-test question first, case study, speaker notes, references slide)
- **Regulatory content integration**: Named laws/regulations that must be accurately represented (e.g., a consumer- or elder-protection statute paired with its implementing rule; an accessibility standard paired with a jurisdiction-specific accessibility law)
- **Exercise/assessment design**: Number of case studies (exactly 3); quiz format (4 options per question, 1 correct answer, answer key + explanations + scoring guide); homework tracking format (column structure, minimum item count)
- **Tone requirements**: Non-corporate and practical; accessible but professional; welcoming yet clinical
- **Reference source requirements**: Named URLs or internal policy documents that must be incorporated; instructions for how to access and use external resources
- **Visual design**: Use of color or visual structure; neutral images; balanced text/visual ratio; section-based layout


## 4. Reference File Requirements

### File Types Needed
- **Internal policy document** (pdf, optional): Organizational policy that the training must accurately reflect (e.g., an internal escalation policy, an internal operations policy); provides the authoritative source for procedure steps and regulatory hooks that must be correctly represented in training content
- **Program curriculum or manual** (URL/pdf): External curriculum or framework being adapted into training format (e.g., a program curriculum manual accessed via an external URL); provides the content to be translated into presentation format
- **Regulatory document** (URL/pdf): Named regulatory framework that must be incorporated (e.g., a consumer- or elder-protection statute and its implementing rule; an accessibility standard and a related national accessibility law); accessed via URL for web research tasks
- **No reference file (pure knowledge)**: Many training materials tasks require no reference files — content derives from domain expertise (e.g., a sales objection-handling framework, a clinical condition's pathophysiology and management, retail sales operations)

### Data Characteristics
Internal policy documents: prose procedure descriptions with numbered steps, named escalation contacts, and specific thresholds or definitions. Program curriculum/manual: chapter-structured educational content with session objectives, activities, and discussion questions. Regulatory documents: formal legal text with definitions, requirements, and prohibitions — must be translated into accessible training language without losing accuracy.

### File Complexity Spectrum
- **Minimal:** No reference files; single-deliverable training document (e.g., Word sales training guide with named sections); content sourced entirely from domain expertise; no regulatory integration required
- **Moderate:** One reference file (internal policy or external program curriculum); dual-deliverable (instructional deck + case studies or exercises); one regulatory framework to integrate; tone/audience calibration required
- **Complex:** Multiple reference sources (internal policy + two named regulatory URLs); three-deliverable package (framework guide + day-to-day checklist + quiz with answer key and scoring rubric); dual-register audience (new hires AND seasoned staff); regulatory accuracy requirement adds verification burden; quiz must have correct answers and explanations

## 5. Output Specification

### Primary Deliverable
- **Format:** PowerPoint (.pptx) for slide-based training; Word (.docx) for coaching guides and training documents; PDF for multi-document training packages
- **Structure:** Varies by format:
  - Slide deck: Title slide → agenda/objectives → content slides (organized by topic) → exercise/practice slide → closing/summary slide → references
  - Training guide: Named sections in order; overview → content area(s) → practice exercises → homework/application tracker
  - Framework document: Purpose/scope → content area(s) → practical guidance → further reading/resources → staff escalation instructions
- **Key quality signals:** All named structural sections present; audience language calibrated correctly (no jargon for novice audiences; appropriate technical depth for professional audiences); regulatory content accurately represented (not oversimplified to the point of inaccuracy); exercise/case study content is realistic and embeds the correct number of specified features (red flags, objection types); word count or slide count within the specified limit; self-contained (usable without the creator present to explain)

### Secondary Deliverables (if any)
- Case study set: fictional but realistic scenarios with embedded features (red flags, objection types, compliance issues) for role-play or discussion
- Quiz with answer key: multiple-choice questions with 4 options, 1 correct answer, explanations per question, scoring guide
- Homework/tracking sheet: structured table for learners to record practice observations (objections heard, type, response, outcome) with due date and name fields
- Day-to-day checklist: condensed practical reference derived from the framework document

### Gold Output Characteristics
A gold-standard training output exhibits: clearly differentiated sections that build progressively (foundational concepts before application); exercises or case studies that embed the correct number of specified features (e.g., a fixed number of fictional accounts, a fixed number of category types); language consistently calibrated to the specified audience (no clinical jargon in patient-facing materials; no corporate coldness in materials for recovery program participants); regulatory content accurately represented and not contradicted anywhere in the deck; assessment components (quiz, homework) that are internally consistent with the instructional content; and a length that fits within the specified constraint without padding or truncation.

## 6. Complexity Knobs

| Knob | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|------|------|--------|------|
| Number of deliverables | Single document | Dual deliverable (instructional + exercises) | Three-document package (guide + checklist + quiz with answer key) |
| Audience calibration | Single, homogeneous audience | Single audience with specified literacy requirement | Dual audience requiring simultaneous accessibility (new hires AND experienced staff; general public AND healthcare professionals) |
| Regulatory integration | None | One named regulation accurately represented | Two or more regulatory frameworks (e.g., two national financial regulators' rules; an accessibility standard plus two jurisdiction-specific accessibility laws) |
| Content sourcing | Pure domain knowledge | One reference file or URL | Multiple URLs + internal policy document + domain knowledge |
| Exercise/assessment design | No exercises required | One exercise type (case study or homework) | Full assessment suite (case studies + role-play + quiz with answer key + scoring rubric) |
| Structural prescription | Open structure | Named sections with content descriptions | Exact item counts per section (e.g., a fixed number of category types, a fixed number of case studies with embedded red flags, a 4-option quiz) |
| Tone constraints | Professional standard | Specified register (non-corporate, plain language) | Dual-register tension (welcoming to vulnerable population AND clinically/legally accurate) |

## 7. Boundary Cases & Adjacent Patterns

**C1 (Standard Operating Procedure):** SOPs and training materials both tell people how to do things, but SOPs are authoritative operational documents (binding on staff) while training materials are pedagogical (designed to build understanding and skill). An SOP for a routine business process (e.g., agreement or contract creation) is C1. A step-by-step instructional guide for new hires covering the same process, with tips, "why it matters" explanations, and troubleshooting advice, is C4. The distinguishing question: is the document designed to be followed as a procedure or to teach someone to understand and execute a procedure?

**C3 (Clinical Guidelines):** Clinical lectures and educational health posters sit at the C3/C4 boundary. When the output is primarily clinical reference content (SBAR template, a condition-specific treatment poster for a health fair), classify as C3. When it is a full instructional package with pedagogical scaffolding — learning objectives, pre-test questions, clinical case study for discussion, speaker notes throughout, references slide — classify as C4. A nursing lecture on a clinical condition with a pre-test, case study, and speaker notes is C4. A prescribing guideline document is C3.

**C2 (Compliance Checklist):** Compliance training materials (C4) teach staff to understand and apply compliance requirements. Compliance checklists (C2) are audit instruments used to evaluate whether compliance occurred. If the deliverable is designed to educate staff about a regulation, it is C4. If the deliverable is designed to audit compliance with a regulation, it is C2.

**D2 (Corporate Strategy/Business Proposals):** Training decks for internal field advisors on investment product suitability (C4) can resemble business presentations (D2). The distinction is purpose: C4 materials are designed to produce learning in the recipient; D2 documents are designed to persuade a decision-maker. A slide deck arguing against a specific investment-product recommendation, prepared for internal field advisors, is C4 (they will learn the argument and apply it in client interactions). A deck proposing a new organizational strategy to leadership is D2.

**B1 (Performance Analysis Presentation):** B1 presentations analyze data from reference files and present findings to stakeholders. C4 training materials teach a skill or body of knowledge. A deck showing Q1 sales performance with charts and narrative is B1. A deck teaching team leaders how to use a new internal planning tool is C4.
