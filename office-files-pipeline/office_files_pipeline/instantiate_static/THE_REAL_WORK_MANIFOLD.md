# What real variation in professional office work looks like

This is population-level grounding — a compressed read on what varies across real workplace tasks once you study a broad cross-section of them. It is not a taxonomy, not a scoring scheme, not instructions. Its purpose is to help you tell apart two very different kinds of breadth when you describe the per-call support for an instantiation: breadth that gives a worker a meaningfully different job, and breadth that merely repaints the same job in different colors.

## The thing easy to miss

A design space with ten dimensions and four values each lists an enormous number of cells. Most of those cells can describe exactly the same underlying activity. An analyst producing a filtered pivot and a short written summary has substantially the same workday whether the persona is framed as peer-to-peer or leadership-facing, whether the jargon is dense or light, whether a regulatory anchor is cited, whether the organization name is invented one way or another. The runner opens the workbook, figures out the right filter, builds the pivot, writes the summary. The surface language changes; the work does not.

The dimensions that *change what the runner has to do* tend to be harder to name. They sit closer to the substance of the job: what the source material forces the worker to grapple with, what the deliverable demands they produce, how much of the answer is already implicit somewhere versus must be constructed, how many places the worker has to look, how much reconciling and judgment the gaps require. You do not reach these by listing more surface knobs; you reach them by reading your specific (metatemplate, file) pair and asking where the actual job could legitimately be different.

## Where real work varies

Professional tasks in this population differ along several axes worth noticing before settling on a support for this call. These are descriptions of how real work actually differs from itself, not a required list.

*How the deliverable relates to the source.* Some tasks produce an artifact from scratch with no source file — the prompt is the whole input and professional knowledge is the substrate. Some take a single source and reformulate it — a raw log into an executive recap, shorthand clinical notes into a polished report. Some fill a template the worker is given, pulling content from elsewhere into pre-arranged structure. Some aggregate across multiple references carrying different kinds of information. A task where a worker reconciles three source voices and decides which to privilege is structurally different work from a task where the worker reads one spreadsheet and summarizes it, which differs again from inventing an artifact purely from professional knowledge.

*How much of the answer is latent in the source versus constructed by the worker.* Cases range from "every required value is literally present; the worker copies it out," through "the source carries raw events the worker must group and aggregate," through "the source hints at a pattern the worker must interpret," through "the critical data is embedded as an image the worker must first see," to "no source at all; the worker constructs the artifact from professional knowledge." The runner trace in each case has a different shape. Variation along this axis carries more training signal than almost any other.

*Where the ambiguity lives and how the worker reconciles it.* Some tasks give a complete prompt, a clean source, and a mechanical deliverable. In others the prompt names an entity one way while the reference names it differently; a number in prose conflicts with an implicit rule in a sheet; a column header is misspelled; a rate is implied but never stated; a placeholder partially replaces an original that survives in related documents. Real workers resolve these constantly, choosing which surface is authoritative for which claim. Whether a task exposes the worker to this friction is a live axis, not a defect. Instantiations that are uniformly pristine produce a runner who never learns to reconcile.

*How the worker handles what the prompt leaves open.* Prompts under-specify in specific directions. They leave the worker to determine which items from a menu to include and why; what the professionally correct quantity is when a vague term has a specific conventional meaning; which of several valid resolutions to a constraint set to commit to; what format choices within granted latitude best serve the deliverable's purpose; which of two plausible readings of scope to honor. Each of these exercises a different capacity. The balance shapes how much of the runner's behavior is execution of explicit instructions versus domain-grounded judgment.

*How inputs relate to each other when there is more than one.* The useful axis when multiple references are present is not their count but their relationship: do they say the same thing in different words, do they cover complementary facets that combine cleanly, or do they partly contradict in ways that demand priority reasoning? Add to this whether the output itself must carry internal cross-references: multi-artifact deliveries often demand consistency the rubric assumes without stating, and keeping that consistency is work distinct from producing any single piece. Synthesis under agreement and synthesis under tension produce very different runner traces even when surface complexity looks similar.

*Where latitude lives and where it is pinned.* Real prompts grant specific kinds of freedom — choice among acceptable forms, layout and aesthetic decisions, selection of exemplars — while pinning other things exactly — the name of a specific entity, a deadline, a structural section, a numeric threshold. What is free and what is pinned varies widely. Two tasks of similar surface complexity can have their anchors on completely different surfaces: one pinning a quantitative result and leaving narrative open, another fixing narrative coverage and leaving numbers latitude. Which surfaces are anchored changes what the runner must optimize against.

## Calibrating a candidate dimension

A practical check for any dimension you are considering: picture two neighboring values on it and ask whether the runner opens different files, consults different parts of the source, makes different judgments, or produces a structurally different deliverable. If any of those is yes, the dimension is behavioral. If the only differences are wording of the persona, presence or absence of a framing sentence, or choice of industry labels, the dimension is likely cosmetic.

A related check: a scalar change ("ten invoices versus fifty invoices") is not the same as a shape change ("ten invoices from one vendor with consistent formatting versus ten invoices from three vendors with conflicting formats and one that is a scanned image"). The first inflates cell count without diversifying work; the second requires the runner to do something different.

Dimensions that pass these checks are rarer than the easy ones and usually closer to the specifics of the source and the metatemplate. They reward careful reading more than clever listing.

## What the source pins and what it leaves free

Some breadth is not available for a given call. A tabular data source pins the deliverable to tabular or tabular-derived form; forcing a slide-deck output makes the task synthetic. A patient roster pins the entity format. A creative brief with stem files pins the modality. A policy document pins the worker's role to someone authorized to interpret it. Stage 1's job is not to force variation where the source does not support it; it is to find the axes where variation is genuine for this specific pair.

Dimensions that feel pinned but are often actually free: the trigger — the plausible business reason the task arrives on the worker's desk now — is rarely fixed by the source; the same file can legitimately arrive for many different reasons, and each reason reshapes the work. The scope ceiling, the consistency burden, the share of the output that is professional judgment versus rule-execution — these often have genuine room even when the source seems to constrain everything.

## Realism boundaries

Breadth is only useful if it stays inside the distribution of real work. Two common ways to overreach: pushing a dimension to a value the occupation does not actually encounter, or combining dimensions into a cell no real worker ever occupies. Both fabricate outliers that read as synthetic. The aim is the full natural range, not beyond it. When a value stretches the occupation's realistic work, the question is whether a worker in that role genuinely sees such cases a non-trivial fraction of the time. If yes, it is a legitimate edge worth including. If no, the cell adds count without adding signal.

Real professional work carries real imperfection — prompts whose values conflict with reference documents, sources with embedded images or odd cells, filenames with authoring residue, domain-conventional answers that override prompt-literal readings, partial contradictions that require priority reasoning. These are texture, not defects. Their moderate presence across the corpus is part of what makes the training signal useful; their complete absence is itself a distributional distortion.

## The shape of useful breadth

The cell-count floor is easy to satisfy, and satisfying it tells you nothing about whether the design space is diverse in the way that matters. What matters is that the dimensions in the support, when sampled, place the stage-2 agent in a meaningfully different authoring position from one cell to the next — facing a different source-deliverable relationship, a different amount of latent versus constructed answer, a different kind of ambiguity, a different balance of pinned and free surfaces, a different reconciliation demand. When dimensions like these are active, the population of stage-2 tasks spans the actual manifold of real work and the resulting runner traces diverge in structure across cells — some opening three files and cross-referencing, some producing directly from one source, some disambiguating a conflict before touching a deliverable, some drafting and revising prose, some computing with rule-table checks. When the active dimensions instead vary the prose around an otherwise-identical task, the population clusters around a single underlying job repainted many times and the runner corpus reflects that clustering.
